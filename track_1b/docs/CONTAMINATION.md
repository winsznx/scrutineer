# Contamination check

Generated by `scrutineer contamination` on 2026-10-15. Machine-readable results are in `docs/contamination.json`. Items file sha256 `fe3ee9af2f440fa314554c0201e5fff83145bbe3e49bcb5e4e7400b86e1625ee`.

The benchmark asks about public facts: official vote titles, dates and results. Those facts are on swissvotes.ch and admin.ch and are almost certainly in any large web crawl, so this check does not try to show that the facts are absent. It asks two narrower questions. First, did the source sites opt out of AI crawling, which would have removed them from the Apertus pretraining data? Second, does the wording of our prompts, which we wrote for this benchmark, appear in the multilingual web text Apertus draws on?

## 2. Prompt overlap with FineWeb-2

Every prompt in German, French and Italian is split into lowercase word tokens (Unicode `\w+`) and cut into 13-grams. A 13-gram counts as inside the title when all 13 tokens fall within the item's `title` field as it appears in the prompt. Any 13-gram that includes a token of the date line, the `Vorlage:` label or the question counts as touching the template. Items without a title (German F5, which describes the vote by its result) are all template. We then scan the FineWeb-2 sample once per language and record every prompt 13-gram that occurs in at least one document.

Titles are matched in two ways. Verbatim: the title, lowercased and with whitespace collapsed, occurs in the lowercased, whitespace-collapsed document text with no word character on either side. Token sequence: the title's word tokens occur as a contiguous run of document tokens, which ignores punctuation and so also catches titles quoted with «» or typographic quotes instead of the straight quotes the Chancellery uses.

How to read these numbers. A prompt counts as a title-only hit when some 13-token stretch of its title occurs in a sampled document, so long titles can match without the whole title appearing verbatim. That is why the title-only prompt counts can exceed the verbatim title counts. A 13-gram touching the template with only one or two template tokens is a title whose last words are followed by a common word such as an article, and says nothing about our question wording. The template wording itself (the date line, the question and the answer-format instruction) would show up as found 13-grams with many template tokens.

## Sample design and deviation from the PRD

The PRD asked for a 1% sample of FineWeb-2 per language. The German, French and Italian configs are roughly 770, 540 and 360 GB of Parquet, so 1% is about 17 GB of downloads. This check ran on a laptop with about 12 GB of free disk and under 1 MB/s of bandwidth, so we scanned a fixed sample of 100,000 documents per language instead. That is a small fraction of a percent of each config. A zero in the template column is therefore evidence about this sample, and an upper bound on overlap elsewhere only in the loose sense that template text found nowhere here is unlikely to be common in the full corpus.

The sample is reproducible from the seed (0). For each language a `random.Random(f"{seed}:{config}")` generator picks one train shard, then picks whole 1,000-row row groups from that shard in random order until the target is reached (the last group is truncated). Shards are read at a pinned dataset revision with HTTP range requests, so only the Parquet footer and the `text` and `url` column chunks of the chosen row groups are transferred, and nothing is written to disk. Shard paths, row group indices and global row ranges are in `contamination.json` under `corpus.languages.<lang>.sample`.

FineWeb-2 is a proxy for the Apertus multilingual data. Apertus filtered it further (the opt-out lists above, among other steps), so documents in our sample may not have been seen by the model, and the model saw documents we did not sample.
